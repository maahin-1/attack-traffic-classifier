# CSE-CIC-IDS2018 compatibility review

## Scope fixed before external model outcomes

The 14 February 2018 publisher CSV is designated development-only for schema, labels and data-quality inspection. The 15 and 16 February 2018 daily CSVs are proposed final evaluation blocks; their identities, archive hashes, compatibility, row rules and availability must be checked and fixed before final evaluation. No final-day outcomes may guide those decisions. Days are separate time blocks within the same research environment, not independent datasets. Other days are not implicitly included. If additional adaptation or model selection uses final-day outcomes, those days no longer qualify as held out.

Publisher documentation: [UNB dataset](https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/ids-2018.html) and [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/cse-cic-ids2018/). The publisher requires citing the dataset and linking the registry. Public infrastructure and synthetic scenarios restrict conclusions about real-world performance.

## Development-source inspection

The official 14 February CSV object reports 358,223,333 bytes, last modified 11 October 2018, and ETag `46c0f45f8e6fe1edf1f08487448c102f-22` (multipart ETag, not an assumed MD5 checksum). A 4,096-byte ranged request returned HTTP 206 and its header. Its column names include aliases corresponding to the canonical 27 inputs, as well as `Label`, `Timestamp`, `Dst Port` and `Protocol`; latter metadata must never become model features. Header similarity alone does not prove compatible extraction semantics, units, or label quality. Only this development object's header was inspected; no external model outcomes were calculated.

The proposed compatibility and acquisition checks below must complete before scoring: record local whole-file SHA-256 and source evidence; verify the 27 aliases one-to-one, ordering, units and definitions against actual upstream CICFlowMeter implementations; define explicit label mappings and unknown-label behavior; account for malformed rows, embedded headers, duplicates and nonfinite values; independently verify final object schemas and provenance; and freeze final inclusion/exclusion policies. Check capture dates and potential overlap separately from equality of feature vectors. Fail closed for unresolved semantic mismatches, rather than implicitly imputing or changing model features.

The development CSV has now been downloaded from the publisher's AWS bucket. Its 358,223,333 bytes have SHA-256 `acff8bc61376ee031d80878ee6099e0b1a87a1bd711d8068298421418c9f8147`; see `data/cicids2018/acquisition-development.json`. This is a locally calculated integrity fingerprint, not an authenticated publisher checksum.

Full development inventory: 1,048,575 data rows: 667,626 benign, 193,360 FTP-BruteForce and 187,589 SSH-Bruteforce. All 27 proposed aliases occur exactly once in the header. Five rows have invalid flow-duration values under the existing finite/nonnegative-duration check; no malformed-width records or embedded repeated headers were found. Counts are source labels, not verified ground truth. These checks do not establish semantic equivalence. See `artifacts/ids2018-development-inventory.json`; `attack_traffic/inspect_ids2018.py` is restricted to the designated development filename and was verified with a synthetic test. No model was scored.

UNB describes CICFlowMeter-V3 and first-packet forward orientation, but does not identify the exact code revision/configuration producing the CSV. Related [CICFlowMeter BasicFlow source](https://github.com/ahlashkari/CICFlowMeter/blob/master/src/main/java/cic/cs/unb/ca/jnetpcap/BasicFlow.java) and the [corrected-flow implementation](https://github.com/GintsEngelen/CICFlowMeter/blob/master/src/main/java/cic/cs/unb/ca/jnetpcap/BasicFlow.java) support payload-based packet statistics and microsecond timing in those versions, but do not prove exact 2018 producer behavior or equivalent flow reconstruction. Mappings remain provisional and final scoring is blocked until the remaining evidence is resolved or a separately documented qualified cross-version robustness protocol is defined. [Correction researchers](https://intrusion-detection.distrinet-research.be/CNS2022/CSECICIDS2018.html) also document possible ineffective attacks and label problems, including the development day. Labels must not be represented as unquestionable ground truth.
